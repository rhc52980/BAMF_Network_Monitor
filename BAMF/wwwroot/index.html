Switches and routers

How your network is cabled, for the Map. BAMF can't find this out itself: ARP shows which devices are on a network, not which port they're plugged into, and budget smart switches don't share their MAC table. So this is your own record, and nothing here connects to a switch. Changes apply straight away. To say where a device is plugged in, use Plugged into… in its ⋯ menu.

Names from your router

Your router knows most devices by the name they asked for, or one you gave them in its own pages. With Bamf:RouterImport set in appsettings.json (OpenWrt, OPNsense, pfSense or UniFi), BAMF reads that list every hour and uses it for any device with no other name.

Check on GreyNoise ↗Has your home's address been seen scanning the internet?

GreyNoise runs sensors all over the internet and records every address that scans them. This checks your home's public address: if it shows up, something behind it has been probing the internet, often a hacked camera, NAS or router in a botnet. It can't say which device, only your address as a whole.

The link checks whatever network this browser is on, so it's only right from home, not from work or over a VPN. If your internet provider shares one address between many homes (carrier-grade NAT), a neighbour's device can flag you. And clean isn't proof: GreyNoise only knows about scanning, not a device quietly sending your data elsewhere.

The link opens GreyNoise's page in a new tab and BAMF sends nothing. BAMF can also check once a day by itself: switch on Check your public address with GreyNoise under Settings → Behaviour.

Network cards

Each network card has its own MAC address, so BAMF sees a machine with a card on each of your networks as a device per card. BAMF's own server is the usual one. Combine them and they're one device: it answers on every card's addresses, and it's drawn once on the Map. Where it's plugged in and what it's the gateway of go with the device you combine into. Separate a card to make it a device of its own again; nothing about it is lost.

Gateway

Tick an address to declare this device the gateway of that address's network. On its own, BAMF only knows a gateway from this machine's routing table: the default gateway of the network BAMF itself routes through, and none for the others. A router with an address on several networks is the gateway of each; tick each one, and the Map draws those networks together, hanging off the router. Each network has one gateway, so ticking this replaces any other declared there.

Switch

If the switch has an address BAMF sees, pick it as its device: the Map then shows the switch online or offline, and its network comes from that device. Leave it at None for an unmanaged switch, or one on an address BAMF doesn't scan.

An access point has no ports to fill in: devices reach it over the air. Give it a wireless SSID for each network it broadcasts, then tick the devices on each SSID. On the Map the SSIDs hang off the access point, with a lightning bolt to each device.

A wireless SSID is a network an access point broadcasts, like "Home" or "Guest". Pick the access point, and the network the SSID puts its devices on. Then tick the devices that connect to it.

A VPN is a device, like a router, and can answer on several addresses. Pick it as its device, and record what it's plugged into. Then tick its clients: devices on any network, each with however many addresses it has. A VPN is never a network's gateway and doesn't join networks together; on the Map its clients hang off it on dotted tunnel lines.

A virtual switch lives inside a machine: Proxmox's vmbr0, ESXi's vSwitch0, a Hyper-V external switch. Pick that machine. Its VMs' traffic leaves through that machine's cable, so on the Map it hangs off the machine, and Find port on a VM pulses the machine's switch port.
