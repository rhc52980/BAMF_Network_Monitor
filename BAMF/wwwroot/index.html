Security

Sign-in

Main password Opens everything. A browser that signs in with it stays signed in for 30 days; scripts, Home Assistant and other BAMF servers send it with each request, as they always have.
View-only password Optional, for family or a wall display: it opens the same dashboard, but nothing can be changed, scanned or woken. Only counts alongside a main password.
HTTPS

Encrypts the connection to the dashboard, so the password and everything on the page can't be read by anyone watching the network. BAMF makes a certificate of its own for this machine. Nothing vouches for it, so each browser warns once, the first time, until you tell it to go on (usually Advanced → Continue), unless the certificate is installed as trusted on that device. Plain HTTP stays on beside it for scripts, Home Assistant and other BAMF servers.

Sends
More destinations

Send alerts to more than one place: your phone and Discord both, or security alerts to one and comings and goings to another. Each has its own format and its own choice of what it gets. Quiet hours hold them all, and the summary when they end goes to each with just its own.

Sends
Scheduled report

A summary to every destination that gets Reports: how many devices are up, what's new, what went away, the flakiest device, the longest offline, the least reliable, any watch alerts, and the top talkers. Daily covers the last 24 hours, weekly the last 7 days, and monthly the last 30 — which also totals up any internet outages, if the internet watch is on.

Unusual activity

BAMF learns what's normal for each device from its own history, and says when something isn't: a device that's almost always on has been off far longer than it ever is; a device came on at an hour it's never on; or a device is answering several times slower than it usually does. Each device needs two weeks of history first. Alerts go to the destinations with Unusual activity ticked under Sends, and quiet hours hold them like any other. What it's noticed is on the Activity tab, where That's normal stops a device being flagged for the same thing for 30 days.

Alert rules and quiet hours

Alerts of your own: a device or a group offline for longer than you'd like, one coming back, or one online when it shouldn't be. Each fires once per occurrence, to the webhook below. Quiet hours hold every alert and deliver them as one summary when the quiet ends.

Switches and routers

How your network is cabled, for the Map. BAMF can't find this out itself: ARP shows which devices are on a network, not which port they're plugged into, and budget smart switches don't share their MAC table. So this is your own record, and nothing here connects to a switch unless you switch on its Counters, which read a managed switch's traffic per port. Changes apply straight away. To say where a device is plugged in, use Plugged into… in its ⋯ menu.

Other BAMF servers

Another BAMF, at the cabin or the office, read once a minute. Its devices show here under networks named after it; nothing here is ever changed there. Its view-only password is all it needs.

Inbound webhooks

A script, Home Assistant or a phone shortcut can start a scan with POST /api/hooks/scan, or wake a device with POST /api/hooks/wake/{mac}. With a token, they need it (as X-BAMF-Token or ?token=) instead of the password.

Back up the database

Everything BAMF knows, as one file: every device and its history, the names, notes and tags, the Map, the floor plans, alert rules, and the settings saved on this page. It's taken while BAMF keeps running, so there's no need to stop anything first. The file includes your saved webhook URL and the passwords BAMF uses for MQTT and other BAMF servers, so keep it somewhere private.

Backups on this machine

The nightly ones, the updaters' from before each update, and the ones kept before a restore. Restore puts one back, the same as restoring from a file, and keeps what it replaces.

Restoring replaces everything here with what's in the file. BAMF checks it's a sound BAMF backup first, and keeps the database it replaces in backups beside it, so a restore can be undone. The Windows and Linux updaters keep a copy from before each update there too.

Port scan results
Network cards

Each network card has its own MAC address, so BAMF sees a machine with a card on each of your networks as a device per card. BAMF's own server is the usual one. Combine them and they're one device: it answers on every card's addresses, and it's drawn once on the Map. Where it's plugged in and what it's the gateway of go with the device you combine into. Separate a card to make it a device of its own again; nothing about it is lost.

Gateway

Tick an address to declare this device the gateway of that address's network. On its own, BAMF only knows a gateway from this machine's routing table: the default gateway of the network BAMF itself routes through, and none for the others. A router with an address on several networks is the gateway of each; tick each one, and the Map draws those networks together, hanging off the router. Each network has one gateway, so ticking this replaces any other declared there.

Find a free address

How full each network is, and the addresses no device has used in the last . Good for picking a static address that won't collide with anything.

BAMF only knows the devices it has seen. Check your router's DHCP range too: a static address should sit outside the range it hands out.

Snooze alerts

While a device is snoozed, its alerts aren't sent: going offline or coming back, the alert rules that match it, and its port watch. Rule and port alerts still appear under Alerts on Activity, marked as not sent. When the snooze ends, a watched device that ended up the other way round from how it started (offline when it was online) gets the alert it missed, so a reboot that never came back isn't lost.

Traffic counters

A managed switch counts every byte through every port, and will tell BAMF over SNMP. Once a minute BAMF reads those counts and gives each port's traffic to the device you recorded on it, so you see what each device really uses, not just what reaches this machine, with no mirror port needed. A port with more than one device on it, or another switch plugged in, can't be split between them and is left out. Needs SNMP v2c switched on in the switch's own settings; BAMF only reads.

Tags

Tags group devices however you like. Filter the device list and the Map by a tag, and show a tag's devices on the Who's home board. Click a tag above to add or remove it.

Device type

In your own words, up to 40 characters: "NAS", "Kids' tablet", "Doorbell". It shows in the device list in place of BAMF's guess, it's what the Device type chips group by, and search finds it. It doesn't change the Map icon unless it names one ("Printer", "NAS"); to pick the icon yourself, use Map icon….

Map icon

Only the picture this device gets on the Map. It doesn't change what the device list calls it: that's Device type… in its ⋯ menu. Automatic uses the icon your device type names (a device typed "NAS" gets the NAS icon), else BAMF's guess. Click one to save.

Ports

Tick the devices that connect to this SSID and save once. Devices from any network can be added. A device already recorded on another switch moves here when you save.

Tick the devices that connect through this VPN and save once. Devices from any network can be added, whatever addresses they have. A device already recorded on a switch moves here when you save.

Tick the devices that run as VMs on this machine and save once. Devices from any network can be added. A device already recorded on another switch moves here when you save.

Pick what's plugged into each port and save once. Location is where that port's cable goes, like "Living Room"; it stays with the port if the device moves. A device already recorded on another switch moves here when you save. A switch plugged into this one is shown on its port; change that in its own settings.

Switch

If the switch has an address BAMF sees, pick it as its device: the Map then shows the switch online or offline, and its network comes from that device. Leave it at None for an unmanaged switch, or one on an address BAMF doesn't scan.

An access point has no ports to fill in: devices reach it over the air. Give it a wireless SSID for each network it broadcasts, then tick the devices on each SSID. On the Map the SSIDs hang off the access point, with a lightning bolt to each device.

A wireless SSID is a network an access point broadcasts, like "Home" or "Guest". Pick the access point, and the network the SSID puts its devices on. Then tick the devices that connect to it.

A VPN is a device, like a router, and can answer on several addresses. Pick it as its device, and record what it's plugged into. Then tick its clients: devices on any network, each with however many addresses it has. A VPN is never a network's gateway and doesn't join networks together; on the Map its clients hang off it on dotted tunnel lines.

A virtual switch lives inside a machine: Proxmox's vmbr0, ESXi's vSwitch0, a Hyper-V external switch. Pick that machine. Its VMs' traffic leaves through that machine's cable, so on the Map it hangs off the machine, and Find port on a VM pulses the machine's switch port.
